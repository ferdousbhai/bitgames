# Independent gameplay judge — 8 October 2026

Audited all 101 projects independently from executable source: 89 generated adventures and 12 original games. Verdicts: 25 rework, 64 remove_candidate, 12 retain_candidate.

These are source-inferred engagement judgments, not browser playtests or proof of what children enjoy. The parent’s direct report is strong evidence that the later catalogue failed its purpose; it does not identify individual favorites. I did not assume that “first 5–6” means the first six catalogue rows.

## Main finding

The generated catalogue mostly multiplies curriculum exercises into store listings. `engine.success` ends each tiny task, disables controls, gives the same celebration and requests the next of five steps. Shared `challenge` modes frequently replace only models, colours and nouns. A pretty vessel, cover or animation cannot turn choosing the requested answer into self-directed play.

The strongest original candidates connect action to a changing world: driving and collision, building then flying a custom rocket, casting and catching discoveries, timing physical cake layers, or creating painted territory. Those deserve actual child play before any final quality guarantee.

Some generated interactions deserve salvage, but not continued release as-is: one music playground, one pattern construction toy, one route adventure, one symmetry/art studio, one concrete quantity playground. Several generated titles have genuine pedagogical distinctions (e.g. fractions versus counts or addition versus sharing); that does not automatically justify separate games.

Retain candidate = prioritize for child play; rework = withhold current form, salvage a distinct affordance into a serious redesign; remove candidate = redundant or thin current listing. All destructive action remains the coordinator’s decision based on combined evidence.

## Release gate recommended

Stop the numerical catalogue target. Before a new listing is kept, observe an age-appropriate child initiating play, understanding the action without a parent reading instructions, experimenting beyond the first correct response, and choosing to replay after the novelty animation. Log friction and abandonment as well as completion. This is an engagement gate, not a promise to maximize screen time or use manipulative rewards.
