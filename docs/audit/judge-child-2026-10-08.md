# Independent child-experience catalogue judgment — 2026-10-08

All 101 projects received individual source-grounded judgments in [the JSON report](judge-child-2026-10-08.json): **12 retain candidates, 18 rework concepts, 71 removal candidates**. None of the 89 generated titles earns an unconditional retain recommendation from this lens. The 12 original titles remain candidates because their direct movement, physics, creation, collection or recognisable memory-game loops provide better reasons to keep playing. This does not establish that all 12 meet the owner’s standard or identify which first 5–6 their children liked.

## What changed the experience

The dominant generated-game structure is “hear an adult-authored target, operate a small answer board, submit or choose the right answer, receive praise, press Next”, repeated five times. In `runtime/engine.js:success`, solving freezes action buttons and adds Next/Finish. Changing scene props, word lists, sound frequencies or fraction pictures mostly does not change this loop. An answer being educationally correct is different from an action being interesting to perform.

Many titles promise play the implementation does not offer: Cloud Number Race has no race, Deep Sea Divers no controlled diver, Rainbow Quilt no kept quilt, Weather Wardrobe no free dressing, Butterfly Life Lab no caterpillar tending, and Paint a Planet no child-owned world. This gap weakens pretend play and discovery even when the models are tidy.

The small fixed banks exhaust rapidly: four animals/tracks, four life stages repeated at every difficulty, six spelling words, four weather situations, or six yes/no experiments. Difficulty and shuffling sometimes change practice demands, but do not create a new reason to visit the same toy. Children who cannot read receive speech support, which is valuable, but must still follow adult-shaped instructions and find answer/action controls rather than discover a mostly self-explanatory world.

Recent composer modes in echo/pitch/rhythm and free-building mode in town/castle genuinely add agency; they were inspected and credited. They do not justify releasing many musical reskins or make 3–4 fixed building pads into a sustained creative town. One strong musical playground and one purposeful construction toy would be better candidates than the current count of variants.

## Interpretation for the wipe decision

Treat the 71 removal candidates as the strongest source-supported candidates for removal from this catalogue. The 18 rework judgments are preservation of ideas, not permission to leave weak games listed; withdraw/defer their current versions unless a demonstrably better game is built. Rework concepts include connected-action material such as pollination navigation, actual mixing, frog/butterfly transformation, magnet/water testing, expressive music, and real tangram manipulation. They need redesigned play, not another cover pass.

The original games are materially stronger baselines because children steer, pop, build a rocket, roll paint, cast a line, time a cake drop or bowl; outcomes arise from those actions. Learning can live inside those loops rather than repeatedly interrupting them. Memory Match is a simpler bounded puzzle, but is already the canonical pair game; four new pair decks need not become four independent products.

This is **not an observed fun score** and **not deletion authorization or a completed deletion**. No child test, educator assessment, fresh rendered browser play or physical iPad assessment occurred. High confidence flags obvious implementation-level duplication; enjoyment remains an inference. Before preserving/releasing anything, judge voluntary return, understandable first interaction, meaningful choices and replay variety through actual play. “Replace YouTube” should mean worthwhile active play the child chooses, not maximising screen duration or adding compulsion.
