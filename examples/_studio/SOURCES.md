# Habitat cues

Fish Pond's collection book labels its mixed animal world as pretend. Its specific real-habitat cues were checked against:

- [NOAA Fisheries: Narwhal](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/narwhal): Arctic Ocean habitat.
- [Florida Museum: Orange clownfish](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/orange-clownfish/): tropical marine reefs and sea-anemone associations.
- [USGS: Habitat Suitability Index Models — Rainbow trout](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/habitat-suitability-index-models-rainbow-trout): freshwater river and lake habitat.

The book does not infer a species or habitat from a generic toy's appearance. Its rainbow-trout note allows sea-run forms rather than implying that every rainbow trout stays in freshwater.

## Fish Pond field guide

Each caught creature in the book shows and reads one short fact (`fish-pond/public/js/field-guide.js`). The narwhal's tusk fact uses the NOAA narwhal page above; the others were checked against:

- [Pennsylvania Sea Grant: Goldfish fact sheet](https://seagrant.psu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/PA-Sea-Grant-AIS-fact-sheet-Goldfish-2023.pdf): freshwater fish kept in ponds and aquariums.
- [UT Marine Science Institute: A swell adaptation](https://utmsi.utexas.edu/science-and-the-sea/print-article/a-swell-adaptation/): pufferfish inflate by gulping water when threatened.
- [NOAA: Blue crab information sheet](https://www.st.nmfs.noaa.gov/Assets/Nemo/documents/lessons/Lesson_21/Lesson_21-Blue_crab_information_sheet.pdf): crabs walk sideways on their walking legs.
- [National Park Service, Pictured Rocks: Reptiles](https://www.nps.gov/piro/learn/nature/reptiles.htm): pond turtles bask on logs to warm up.
- [Smithsonian Ocean: Octopuses, squids and relatives](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/octopuses-squids-and-relatives): octopuses have eight arms and live in the sea.
- [NOAA Ocean Explorer: Bioluminescence fact sheet](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/edu/materials/bioluminescence-fact-sheet.pdf): some jellyfish make their own light.
- [Smithsonian Ocean: Whales](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/marine-mammals/whales): whales breathe air through blowholes on top of the head.

Blue Fish, Golden Fish, the rubber duck, the old boot and the treasure chest are not real animals: the book calls them pretend, a toy or rubbish that we take out of the water.
